Same numbers. Opposite conclusion.

Free cash flow 2,409. Net debt 13,505. Diluted shares 312.2 million. Value per share 114.93. Not a target.

Free cash flow is after maintenance and growth capex only. FY2025 cash used in acquisitions and investments, net, was 1,430. Cash after buying other routes is 2,409 − 1,430 = 979 if that deal spend is required to keep the 3.5% revenue growth. The long memo’s 2,409 overstates owner earnings if Republic has to keep writing checks of that size for density.

Net debt of 13,505 on operating income 3,302 plus DDAA 1,928 is about 2.6× EBITDA. That is serviceable. It is not a fortress once acquisition cash is added back to the uses of free cash flow.

The share count is the FY2025 diluted average, not a later print. A smaller later count would lift value per share without a new cash-flow fact. This pack does not do that.

Customer language is not a concentration number. The 10-K gives a dispersion sentence, not a largest-customer percentage. WM’s largest customer was less than 5% of 2025 revenue. That cap is not imported onto Republic.

The 114.93 versus 209.96 gap is the bear case on the model, not the bull case on the stock. A one-stage Gordon on current free cash flow at 7.5% and 2.5% will undershoot any compounder the market believes can grow faster or earn a tighter cost of capital. If those yellow cells are wrong the other way, value per share falls further.
